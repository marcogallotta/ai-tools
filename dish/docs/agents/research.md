# Research and epistemic sufficiency

Use this shared procedure for substantial research, investigation, or design and before material
semantic Implementation authoring. It creates no Research role, approval gate, lifecycle state,
source-policy authority, or numeric confidence threshold. Existing role, task, Review, and
Integration authority remains unchanged.

## Shared sufficiency invariant

Before crossing a material semantic commitment boundary, the acting agent must be able to state:

- the operator or task outcome it is serving;
- the relevant current mechanism, behavior, or requirement model;
- the material evidence supporting that model;
- the material parts it still does not understand; and
- whether an unresolved gap could change scope, architecture, semantics, operator behavior, safety,
  tests, or acceptance.

Classify every material understanding gap:

- **RESOLVABLE FROM AUTHORITY/EVIDENCE** — investigate first; do not ask Marco.
- **LOCAL-ONLY EVIDENCE REQUIRED** — use the smallest bounded local investigation through the
  existing host-routing contract.
- **MARCO-ONLY MATERIAL UNKNOWN** — stop only the affected semantic decision and ask Marco the
  smallest concrete question.
- **NON-MATERIAL / SAFE DEFAULT** — continue only under an already-authorized safe default; state
  the assumption when useful.
- **UNRESOLVED MATERIAL UNKNOWN** — do not cross the affected semantic boundary.

Self-reported or numeric confidence never overrides missing material evidence. Uncertainty is not
itself a blocker: first resolve everything available from current task, repository, architecture,
tests, GitHub, Asana, runtime evidence, and authorized primary sources.

When only Marco can settle a material fact, use this concise packet:

- **MY CURRENT UNDERSTANDING:** what the agent believes;
- **WHERE MY UNDERSTANDING IS WEAK:** the exact gap;
- **WHY IT MATTERS:** the semantic decision that could change;
- **QUESTIONS:** the smallest concrete questions needed.

## Research procedure

1. **Frame the decision first.** Record the original outcome/problem, exact research question,
   decision or design informed, and non-goals. Research cannot widen the objective or create
   authority.
2. **Ground in Dish first.** Inspect enough current repository and live authority to explain the
   mechanism being changed. Do not benchmark an imagined system.
3. **Keep claims epistemically distinct.** Separate Dish-verified fact, Marco fact/constraint,
   external primary evidence, inference/hypothesis, and unknown. Preserve corrections when evidence
   disproves a hypothesis.
4. **Use the knowability route.** Read or measure obtainable evidence; use current first-party
   sources for external behavior; route genuine local-only evidence through existing host rules;
   ask Marco only for material operator/private facts only he can settle.
5. **Prove environment applicability.** For a material mechanism, classify required capabilities as
   VERIFIED AVAILABLE, VERIFIED UNAVAILABLE, or UNKNOWN against the actual target. A required
   unknown leaves a hypothesis, not a recommendation; verified unavailability rejects it.
6. **Choose appropriate comparators.** For substantial agentic workflow/design work, include
   genuinely agentic comparators where relevant and record what transfers, what does not, and the
   material operating-model mismatch. Apply current source-governance dispositions; absence of a
   disposition never means ALLOWED.
7. **Scale depth to consequence and uncertainty.** Narrow facts stay narrow. Material process,
   architecture, or operator-model work earns internal grounding, current primary evidence,
   relevant comparison, and counterevidence. No fixed source quota applies.
8. **Challenge the emerging answer.** Test contrary evidence, cheaper/no-new-gate alternatives,
   failure cases, operator-cost asymmetry, and whether citations actually support the Dish
   inference.
9. **Preserve current truth and chronology separately.** Current Asana notes hold current
   conclusions; append-only evidence preserves experiments, rejected hypotheses, and corrections.
   Design-bearing output continues to use the one Review V2 generation lineage.
10. **Leave a durable handoff.** Record conclusions, evidence pointers, corrected/rejected
    hypotheses, unresolved classified gaps, applicable comparator judgments, and the exact next
    decision or action. A successor must not silently convert an inherited gap into an assumption.

- Anthropic research — FOLLOW for research methodology and uncertainty/clarification behavior.
- Factory Missions — FOLLOW/TRANSLATE for durable agent state, orchestration, independent

- Cursor and Devin — TRANSLATE for codebase research/planning; their human plan-review assumptions

These are evidence-dated comparator judgments, not a company whitelist or source-policy
disposition. Reassess them when current evidence changes.

## Implementation boundary

Before material semantic authoring, Implementation applies the shared invariant to the accepted
task/design and current repository mechanism. It must understand the requested outcome, the
semantic behavior to change or preserve, the current mechanism, the evidence that distinguishes a
correct result from plausible-but-wrong code, and every remaining material gap.

Implementation resolves gaps from supplied authority first. Routine bounded mechanics, naming, and
refactors consistent with the accepted outcome remain agent-owned. A material unresolved assumption
cannot be converted into code, tests, or publication:

- an answer available in task/repository evidence is researched, not escalated;
- genuine local-only evidence uses the existing remote/local host boundary;
- a Marco-only material product or operator fact pauses only the affected semantic decision and
  uses the bounded packet above;
- a contradiction between accepted design and current repository reality that changes semantics
  returns to the design owner rather than being silently redesigned;
- takeover or compaction preserves unresolved gaps in durable task/PR evidence; and
- green tests do not establish understanding when they do not prove the disputed semantic
  requirement.

Before declaring a candidate review-ready, explain how its semantics satisfy the accepted outcome
and what evidence proves that. If a material gap remains, keep the affected authoring path
unresolved rather than handing Review a plausible candidate as settled.
