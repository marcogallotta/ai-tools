Nihari

Beef shank pressure-cooked with spices, then finished on the stovetop with controlled atta thickening and fresh ginger.

WHY COOK IT
Learn a gelatin-rich extraction gravy distinct from emulsion and reduction.

CAN I COOK IT? Yes.

## QUANTITIES
- Beef shank: 1kg, bone-in if practical
- Oil or ghee: 60ml
- Onion: 200g
- Garlic: 20g
- Ginger: 25g, split cooked and fresh
- Water: 900ml in the Instant Pot, subject to max fill
- Atta: 30g mixed with 90ml cold water
- Fine salt: begin with 11g

Spices (scaled ~2.2x off the source's 1lb/454g meat basis to this task's 1kg shank):
- Fennel: 1 tsp
- Coriander: 1 tsp
- Cumin: 1 tsp
- Black pepper: 1/2 tsp
- Cloves: 7–8
- Green cardamom: 6–7
- Black cardamom: 1 — not in the cited source (which points to a separate masala recipe); kept as a standard nihari-masala inclusion, unconfirmed by this citation
- Cassia: 1 stick, about 7–8cm — corrected from "4cm", which was a likely cm/inch slip against the source's 3–4in stick
- Star anise: 2
- Nutmeg: pinch
- Mace: optional small blade — same unconfirmed status as black cardamom
- Kashmiri chilli: 2–2.5 tsp — consolidates the source's paprika + chilli powder + crushed chilli into one form
- Turmeric: 1/2 tsp

## HOW TO COOK IT
1. Toast and grind whole spices.
2. Brown beef; cook onion deep golden; add garlic and cooked ginger.
3. Add spices, water and beef.
4. Pressure-cook on high for 50 minutes; natural release 20 minutes, then vent.
5. Add 8–10 minutes only if shank remains tight.
6. Simmer stovetop and add slurry gradually until lightly coating; unused slurry is expected. (The source instead does a second low-pressure Instant Pot cycle before an optional stovetop reduction; this task uses a plain stovetop simmer instead, to avoid double pressure-cycling — a disclosed simplification, not a sourced match.)
7. Simmer 10–15 minutes to cook out flour; adjust salt and water.
8. Finish with ginger julienne.

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE
Beef is collapsingly tender. Gravy is gelatinous and flowing: it coats a spoon but pours back rather than standing as paste. Atta is lump-free and cooked through; a controlled surface sheen and fresh ginger remain.

Visual reference — https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/nihari/ — Observe smooth slurry, gradual addition, a thickened but flowing finish, surface fat and fresh ginger. Ignore mutton, yogurt, stovetop timing, flour amount and spice formula.

## WATCH OUT FOR
Do not use flour to compensate for weak extraction, add all slurry automatically or carry stovetop water into the pressure cooker.
