Chana/chole repeat
Chana/chole repeat — controlled hydration

Kabuli chickpeas in a medium-bodied onion-tomato gravy, using reserved chickpea liquor to stop the cook before it drifts into dry bhuna.

WHY COOK IT
Keep chana/chole as a distinctive repeatable legume dish while correcting the prior insufficient-liquor problem.

CAN I COOK IT? Yes — if chickpeas are soaked.

## QUANTITIES
- Dried kabuli chickpeas: 300g, soaked overnight
- Reserved cooking liquor: at least 500ml
- Neutral oil: 45ml
- Onion: 300g
- Garlic: 18g
- Ginger: 18g
- Green chilli: 1–2
- Pelati tomatoes: 400g
- Cumin seed: 1.5 tsp
- Coriander seed: 2 tsp
- Turmeric: 1/2 tsp
- Kashmiri chilli: 1 tsp
- Hing (asafoetida): a small pinch (about 1/8 tsp), bloomed in the oil
- Black cardamom: 1 pod, bruised
- Amchur: begin with 1 tsp
- Marco's own garam masala: optional 1 tsp
- Fine salt: begin with 10g

## HOW TO COOK IT
1. Pressure-cook chickpeas until fully tender and reserve the liquor.
2. Bloom cumin and hing together, then cook onion soft to light golden.
3. Add garlic, ginger, chilli, black cardamom and ground spices.
4. Add tomato and cook cohesive, stopping before dry bhuna.
5. Add chickpeas and 350ml liquor. Simmer to medium-bodied, flowing but clinging.
6. Add more liquor whenever pastiness advances beyond target.
7. Finish with amchur and optional own garam masala; remove the black cardamom pod; rest warm.

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE
Chickpeas are fully tender and suspended in cohesive gravy that coats but still settles and flows between them. It is neither watery soup nor dry masala crust; most oil remains integrated.

Visual upper boundary — https://www.seriouseats.com/channa-masala-recipe — Observe chickpeas suspended in integrated tomato-spice gravy. Its moundable final body is the upper thickness boundary: stop earlier and looser. Ignore exact onion, tomato, spice, tea and cooking method.

## WATCH OUT FOR
Reserve liquor before draining, use kabuli rather than kala chana and omit butter from the baseline.
