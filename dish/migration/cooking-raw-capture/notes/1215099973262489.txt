Chettinad chicken
Chettinad chicken — roasted spice and dry-paste system

Chicken cooked in a freshly roasted pepper, fennel and coriander masala with curry leaves and a restrained coconut-onion base.

WHY COOK IT
Add a South Indian roasted-spice and dry-paste contrast to korma and North Indian gravy systems.

CAN I COOK IT? Yes — if curry leaves remain after the higher-priority Sri Lankan task.

## QUANTITIES
- Bone-in chicken: 900g
- Onion: 250g
- Tomato: 250g
- Garlic: 20g
- Ginger: 20g
- Curry leaves: 20
- Coconut: 40g
- Neutral oil: 40ml
- Water: 200ml initially
- Fine salt: begin with 9g

Roast and grind:
- Coriander seed: 2 tbsp
- Fennel seed: 2 tsp
- Cumin seed: 1 tsp
- Black pepper: 2 tsp
- Dried chilli: 8–12g
- Cassia: 3cm
- Cloves: 4
- Green cardamom: 4
- Star anise: 1/2
- Poppy seed: optional 1 tsp
- Turmeric: 1/2 tsp after grinding

## HOW TO COOK IT
1. Roast spices and coconut separately; cool and grind with a little water.
2. Brown chicken lightly and remove.
3. Cook onion and curry leaves until golden; add garlic and ginger.
4. Add tomato and reduce.
5. Fry masala paste until rawness disappears and oil begins showing.
6. Return chicken with water and salt; cover and simmer until tender.
7. Uncover and reduce until masala coats without greasiness.

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE
Spices and coconut are deepened without black scorch. Ground masala coats rather than remaining sandy. The finished gravy is dark and clinging with visible curry leaves and restrained oil; it is neither soupy nor burnt-dry.

Visual reference — https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/chicken-chettinad/ — Observe whole spices roasted, coconut and curry leaves toasted, a smooth ground paste, tomato reduced before paste frying and dark cohesive gravy coating chicken. Ignore kalpasi, cashews, marination, exact ratios and spice formula.

## WATCH OUT FOR
Do not scorch coconut or spices, overdo pepper or make the dish soupy.
