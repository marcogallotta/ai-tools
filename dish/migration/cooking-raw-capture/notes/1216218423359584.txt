Cá kho tộ

Vietnamese caramel-braised fish adapted to locally available whole small mackerel. The wide-pan route preserves kho seasoning and reduction logic without claiming clay-pot equivalence.

WHY COOK IT
Test fish-specific kho structure: nước màu caramel, fish-sauce seasoning, controlled braising and sauce concentration without breaking or overcooking the fish.

CAN I COOK IT? No - awaiting independent verification.

## WHAT TO BUY
Per cook, target 250–320g whole fish:
- 2 fish if about 170g each; or
- 3–4 fish if about 70–80g each

## QUANTITIES
Portions: one fresh portion per cook; plan two separate fresh calibration cooks.
- Whole small mackerel: 250–320g, gutted and cleaned
- Fish sauce: 12ml
- White sugar for marinade: 2g
- White sugar for nước màu: 5g
- Neutral oil: 1 tsp, optional
- Coconut water: about 40ml; water is the fallback
- Garlic: 2 cloves
- Shallot: 1 small
- Black pepper: to taste
- Chilli: optional

## HOW TO COOK IT
1. Dry the fish thoroughly. Keep tiny fish whole.
2. Coat gently with fish sauce and 2g sugar; rest for 10–20 minutes.
3. Cook 5g sugar to dark amber.
4. Add garlic and shallot briefly, then coconut water or water; reduce slightly.
5. Add fish in one layer and spoon sauce over it.
6. Simmer gently, partly covered, without stirring or repeated flipping.
7. Begin checking tiny fish at 8 minutes and 170g fish at 12 minutes. Stop when flesh releases cleanly from the bone.
8. Remove fish if sauce needs further reduction, then glaze briefly or spoon the finished sauce over it.
9. Finish with pepper and optional chilli.

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE
Fish remains intact and moist; skin and backbone support the structure. A dark mahogany, glossy sauce clings around the fish rather than sitting as a pale broth, and caramel and fish sauce balance without excess sweetness.

Visual analogue — https://www.seriouseats.com/vietnamese-dishes-foods-you-should-know-hanoi-saigon-pho-banh-mi-slideshow — Observe the cá-kho-tộ image for mahogany colour, intact fish and concentrated sauce. Ignore the clay pot, larger cut fish and long-braise texture; it does not validate whole tiny-mackerel timing or bone-point doneness.

## WATCH OUT FOR
Do not use skinless fillets as the default, hard-boil or repeatedly flip the fish, hold cooked fish during sauce reduction or substitute coconut milk for coconut water.

## STORAGE
Eat fresh. The two-cook plan is a calibration loop, not leftover storage.
