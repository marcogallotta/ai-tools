Dubu-jorim — Korean braised tofu

Firm tofu browned lightly, then briefly braised in a ganjang, gochugaru, garlic and scallion sauce until seasoned and glossy.

WHY COOK IT
Build Korean browned-tofu jorim technique and, when the homemade ganjang is released for cooking, observe how the young ganjang behaves under a short reduction without onion or heavy sesame masking it.

CAN I COOK IT? No — initial verification pending; young homemade ganjang is not yet available or released for cooking. A light-soy cook is allowed only as a separately recorded technique rehearsal, not as the ganjang assessment.

## QUANTITIES
Portions: 3 banchan servings. This is not a standalone 800kcal / 50g-protein meal; serve with rice and other dishes.
- Firm tofu: 500g, cut into 12–13mm rectangular slices
- Neutral oil: 20ml, divided for two browning batches
- Young ganjang: 45ml, or the saltier-ganjang branch above
- Water: 45ml, or the saltier-ganjang branch above
- White sugar: 5ml / about 4g [approx]
- Korean gochugaru: 5ml / about 2g [approx]
- Garlic: 5ml minced / about 5g [approx]
- Scallion whites and pale greens: 20g, finely sliced
- Scallion dark greens: 10g, finely sliced and held for finishing
- Toasted sesame oil: 5ml
- Toasted sesame seeds: 5ml / about 3g [approx]

## HOW TO COOK IT
1. Drain the tofu for 10 minutes and pat it dry without hard-pressing. Cut into 12–13mm slices.
2. Mix ganjang, water, sugar, gochugaru, garlic and the 20g scallion whites/pale greens. Keep sesame oil, dark scallion greens and sesame seeds separate.
3. Heat the 24cm non-stick pan over medium to medium-high heat. Add 10ml oil and brown half the tofu for about 3–4 minutes per side, until lightly golden and resilient enough to turn without tearing. Repeat with the remaining oil and tofu. Do not chase a hard crust.
4. Arrange all tofu in one shallow layer in the 28cm stainless pan. Pour the sauce over and lift edges so liquid reaches underneath.
5. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium-low heat. Cook 3–4 minutes, spooning sauce over the tops. Turn carefully, then cook another 1–2 minutes.
6. Stop while the sauce is glossy and concentrated but one or two tablespoons still move freely when the pan is tilted. If it is disappearing before the tofu is seasoned, add water 15ml at a time. If using the saltier-ganjang branch and the sauce remains underseasoned, add the reserved ganjang 5ml at a time.
7. Remove from the heat. Add sesame oil, dark scallion greens and sesame seeds. Rest 5 minutes before tasting so surface heat does not hide salt intensity.

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE
The tofu remains intact, lightly golden outside and tender within. The brief braise seasons the surfaces and edges without turning them leathery. The sauce clings in a thin glossy layer but is not reduced to a salty paste. Ganjang, garlic and restrained chilli remain legible; sesame aroma is a finish rather than the dominant flavour.

Visual reference — https://www.koreanbapsang.com/dubu-jorim-korean-braised-tofu/ — Observe 12–13mm tofu slices, light golden browning, sauce spooned over and underneath, intact turning, and a brief 3–4-minute plus 1–2-minute braise. Ignore its full 15ml sesame-oil quantity; this task deliberately uses the lower Maangchi-supported 5ml finish so young ganjang remains easier to assess.

## WATCH OUT FOR
- Do not extend the braise to 6–10 minutes by default; the source-backed 12–13mm construction uses about 4–6 minutes total.
- Do not reduce the pan dry. Add water, not more soy, if evaporation outruns seasoning.
- Do not add sesame oil at the start; prolonged heat dulls it and makes the ganjang assessment less clear.
- Do not treat a light-soy rehearsal as evidence about the homemade ganjang.

## STORAGE
Good warm, cool or at room temperature. Refrigerate up to 3 days with the sauce; bring toward room temperature or rewarm gently so the tofu does not toughen and the sauce does not reduce further.
