Pajeon

Two thin scallion-led pancakes with crisp edges and a soy-vinegar dipping sauce.

WHY COOK IT
Build Korean jeon technique: scallion structure, cold loose batter, pan heat, oil level, crisp edges and dipping-sauce balance.

CAN I COOK IT? No - awaiting independent verification.

## WHAT TO BUY
- Scallions: 180–200g
- Plain flour, if low
- Potato or corn starch, if low

## CHECK BEFORE COOKING
- Use young homemade ganjang in the sauce only if ready; otherwise use a known halal-suitable soy and record the substitution.
- Use fresh scallions with lively greens.

## QUANTITIES
Yield: 2 medium pancakes.

Pancake:
- Scallions: 180–200g, cut to pan length
- Plain flour: 120g
- Potato or corn starch: 40g
- Fine salt: 2g
- Ice-cold water: begin with 190ml; up to 220ml if needed
- Egg: 1, optional
- Neutral oil: 45–60ml across two pancakes

Dipping sauce:
- Young ganjang, Korean soy or halal-suitable light soy: 30ml
- Rice vinegar: 15ml
- Water: 30ml
- Gochugaru: 1/2 tsp, optional
- Garlic: 1 small clove
- Toasted sesame seeds: 1 tsp
- Scallion: 1 tbsp, finely sliced

## HOW TO COOK IT
1. Mix the dipping sauce.
2. Whisk flour, starch and salt. Add egg if using, then 190ml ice water. Keep the batter loose enough to coat lightly.
3. Heat the pan thoroughly and add a visible shallow film of oil.
4. Arrange half the scallions in one layer and pour over half the batter.
5. Cook over medium-high heat until deeply golden and crisp underneath. Flip and finish the second side.
6. Drain briefly on a rack and serve immediately. Repeat.

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE
Scallions remain distinct. Batter is thin and tender inside with crisp oily edges, and the sauce is sharp enough to cut the fry.

Visual reference and free video — https://www.maangchi.com/recipe/pajeon — Observe scallions laid parallel in one layer, batter poured evenly over them, the visible oil film and the crispy golden-brown underside used as the flipping cue. Ignore the source’s doenjang-and-sugar batter, rectangular shape and lack of added starch; it does not validate this task’s flour-starch ratio or dipping sauce.

## WATCH OUT FOR
Do not use warm or thick batter, stack finished pancakes or turn the first baseline into haemul pajeon.

## STORAGE
Eat immediately.

### Research basis
Source-backed dish.
Construction update — Korean Bapsang supports starch-containing batter, optional egg and two large pancakes: one cup
pancake mix or salted flour to 180ml ice water, plus scallions. Its dip is 15ml soy, 10–15ml vinegar
and 15ml water. For a homemade mix, 120g flour plus 30–40g starch is reasonable; add ice water only
to thin-coating consistency and record the actual amount. Dry scallions and use adequate oil.
Sources: <https://www.koreanbapsang.com/haemul-pajeon/>

Dip-sauce citation correction — the task previously cited "House of Nash Eats (2 tablespoons soy : 1
tablespoon vinegar : 1 tablespoon water)" as supporting this task's soy-forward 30:20:10 ratio, with
no URL. Reopened the actual page directly (houseofnasheats.com/korean-pancakes-scallions-pajeon/):
its real dipping sauce is 1 tbsp soy sauce : 1 tbsp water : 1 tsp rice vinegar — soy and water equal,
vinegar the smallest component — the opposite proportional relationship from what was previously
claimed, and closer to the originally-cited Korean Bapsang source's near-equal-parts style
(15ml soy : 15ml water : 12ml vinegar) than to the task's own prior 30:20:10. The dip ratio is
corrected to 30ml soy : 30ml water : 15ml rice vinegar, consistent with both real sources' soy≈water,
reduced-vinegar pattern.
Sources: <https://www.koreanbapsang.com/haemul-pajeon/>, <https://houseofnasheats.com/korean-pancakes-scallions-pajeon/>
